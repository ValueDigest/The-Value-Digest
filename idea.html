Aug 2026 Building Products — Fixtures & Fittings Disclosure: Held
GWA Group Limited
GWA : ASX

A research, design, import and marketing business for bathroom and kitchen fixtures and fittings, sold under well-known brands including Caroma. Trading at 9.6× EV/EBIT with a fully franked 6.6% dividend yield, GWA offers a long, consistent record of profitability and a high shareholder yield, even as growth remains limited.

What does GWA do?

GWA Group is engaged in the research, design, import and marketing of building fixtures and fittings for residential and commercial premises, including sanitaryware, tapware, showers, baths and water management solutions, along with related kitchen, bathroom and laundry accessories. The Group distributes, installs, maintains and repairs these products through various distribution and customer channels across Australia, New Zealand and selected international markets.

Analyst commentary

GWA could lay claim to being one of the most consistent companies on the ASX. Since listing, they have been profitable for several decades dating back to the 1990s, and with this consistent profitability has come significant dividend payments for shareholders. However, consistency isn't always a great thing — in 1997 EPS were 12.4 cents per share, and for FY2025, EPS were 17.4 cents per share (remarkably with little fluctuation in between), which does demonstrate that this company and its management struggle to find growth, or lack a growth mindset. The stock price peaked in 2007 at $5.24 per share. Part of the reason for this may be over-prioritisation on dividend payments, where payout ratios have generally exceeded 80% over the past decade. Conversely, it could be argued that management may recognise that growth options are limited, and the best use of capital is to return it to shareholders.


So why do we like GWA if it can't grow meaningfully? You don't necessarily need growth to get market-like or market-beating returns. With free cash flow yield pushing towards 10%, a dividend yield of 6.6% pre-franking credits, plus the fact that the company has bought back around 4% of its stock (shareholder yield above 10%), it seems reasonable that GWA should be able to produce high-single-digit to 10% returns through all types of economic cycles. Ideally, we would prefer to buy these limited-growth companies at a P/E of 10 or less, but given the current climate — with share markets at record highs and perhaps significantly over-valued — we don't see a lot of great opportunities in this environment, and hence are happy to take the cash this company throws off as part of our portfolio. Next time you're in a public bathroom, there's a good chance you'll notice the Caroma brand on the urinal, taps and so on — a reminder of how ubiquitous this business really is.


One watch-out with GWA is its debt level. While manageable at present, with net interest cover in excess of 9 times, it is interesting that management continue to buy back stock before paying down this debt. With annual interest repayments of around $10M, this represents roughly 6–7% interest on the debt. Given buybacks are unlikely to return significantly better than this, we believe management should prioritise reducing debt and the associated interest burden ahead of further share repurchases at current prices.
